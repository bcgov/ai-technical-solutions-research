Accuracy

Accuracy research helps BC Gov understand whether AI systems are reliable enough for real workflows. We compare outputs, measure failure modes, and document the evidence needed before a model or workflow is scaled.

Why It Matters

Accuracy before adoption

Go / no-go evidence

Accuracy testing helps decide whether an AI workflow should be adopted, changed, handed off, or stopped.

Failure modes

Good demos are not enough. We need to know where a model fails, how often, and what the operational impact is.

Human review burden

Accuracy work should estimate how much human review remains, not only whether the system can produce an answer.

Cost and compute fit

The most accurate option may not be the best operational choice if it is too slow, expensive, or hard to support.

Evaluation Shape

What accuracy work should capture

Representative data

Use real or realistic samples that reflect the forms, documents, images, language, and edge cases teams handle.

Field-level scoring

For document AI, measure exact fields, missing values, hallucinations, parseability, and confidence separately.

Workflow impact

Accuracy should connect to the service workflow: triage speed, review effort, exception handling, and risk.

Repeatable artifacts

Notebooks, scripts, reports, and datasets should make results reproducible and easier to hand off.

How To Use This Pattern

Apply the same evaluation shape to other partner workloads: define the task, collect representative samples, compare model/runtime options, measure accuracy and failure modes, and document operational tradeoffs.
